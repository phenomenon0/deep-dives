GNM, END TO END

One formula. One day. A photo walks in; a face talks back. 

1 · The Linear Thesis

Seventeen thousand eight hundred twenty-one vertices, and not a single neural network runs at render time. That is the entire reason a one-day sprint produced a lab where a photograph becomes a 3D head that lip-syncs to audio, mimics your webcam face, and re-skins itself into five art styles. Google's GNM — the Generative aNthropometric Model, Apache-2.0, released as a quiet repo with 66 stars — is not an AI model in the runtime sense. It is a formula:

the whole modelvertices = template
         + identity   @ identity_basis      # (253) · (253, 17821, 3)
         + expression @ expression_basis    # (383) · (383, 17821, 3)
then linear_blend_skinning(vertices, 4 joints)   # pose correctives: all zeros

Two matrix multiplies and a skinning pass. The 383 expression coefficients are regional and disentangled — 100 per eye, 150 for the lower face (energy-ordered), 32 for the tongue, 1 for the pupils. The tongue block matters more than it looks: ARKit's 52 blendshapes — the industry's lingua franca — have no tongue, which means every ReadyPlayerMe-style avatar physically cannot say "L."

Think of GNM as a mixing desk, not a musician. A neural lipsync model is a session player — you need it present, warmed up, on GPU, every time. GNM is the desk: 636 labelled faders wired directly to skin. Faders don't improvise, which is exactly why you can photograph the desk (bake it), mail it to a browser, and every fader still works. All the intelligence in this lab lives in what moves the faders — and that part you can swap, train, and distill without ever touching the desk.

Linearity cashed out five separate times in one day: (1) morph-target baking is exact, not an approximation — a GLB reproduces the Python model bit-for-bit; (2) fitting a face to landmarks is closed-form ridge regression, no optimizer; (3) a learned retarget matrix bakes into 52 morph targets with zero client code; (4) a training loss in vertex space is one einsum — no differentiable renderer required; (5) the full 383-component basis compresses to ~2 MB sparse fp16 — phone-sized.

Everything below is that one fact, exploited from five directions. Hold onto it — the first thing it buys you is a demo.

2 · Standing It Up

CLONE → VERIFY → BAKE → RENDER

The model weights ship inside the repo — gnm_head.npz, no gated download, no license click-through. First smoke test: load, generate a neutral head and a CVAE-sampled "happy" head, export GLB. It worked on the first run, which for a 4-month-old research repo is its own data point.

The demo strategy follows directly from linearity: compile the formula into a GLB. Pick useful expression directions, evaluate each one's vertex delta once in Python, store the deltas as named morph targets. The browser then "runs GNM" as a weighted sum it was already going to compute for any morph-target mesh. three.js never imports a line of GNM code.

The identity morphs deserve a beat: identity is a separate 253-dim linear space, so a person is 253 numbers, and switching people mid-speech is legal — expression deltas are identity-independent in a linear model. The demo's Randomize Face button while LAM talks is a party trick that is also a correctness proof.

A head that renders is not a head that talks. Talking is where the first real failure lives.

3 · The Sync Problem

The first talk mode cheated: browser TTS plus an amplitude-modulated sine wiggling the mouth morphs on word-boundary events. The user's verdict arrived in four words — "the sync is way off." Correct. Phonemes were fiction; only rhythm was real.

The fix has two halves. The intelligence half: LAM_Audio2Expression (Alibaba, Apache-2.0, 97.9M params, already sitting on disk from earlier avatar work) — a wav2vec2 encoder feeding a streaming decoder that emits 52 ARKit blendshape weights at 30fps from 1-second audio chunks. A tiny websocket server loads it once on the 3090 and streams frames.

The architecture half is the one worth stealing:

zen · one clock to rule them
Never schedule mouth frames on wall time. The browser indexes the frame array by audio.currentTime × 30 every render tick, lerping between neighbors. Audio drift, GC pauses, tab throttling — none of it can desynchronize the mouth, because the mouth reads the audio element's own clock. Sync stopped being a tuning problem and became a structural guarantee.

First playback: pipeline mechanically perfect — 240 frames for 8 seconds, clock advancing, playback auto-stopping. And the face barely moved, then moved wrong. Two bugs, and the second one taught the day's biggest lesson.

Bug one was coverage: LAM's hardest-driven channels (mouthShrugUpper mean 0.38, mouthLowerDown 0.31) simply had no corresponding morphs in the v1 bake — most of the signal was discarded on arrival. Bug two was the shapes themselves.

4 · The Disfigured Mouth

"It doesn't look like lips." The hand-built ARKit shapes produced a mouth that articulated on schedule and looked like a wound. The instinct that caused it is worth dissecting, because it is the default instinct of anyone building blendshapes by hand.

✗ vertex-space scoping

Take a full-face expression, multiply the delta by a lip-region vertex mask. Feels surgical.

Why it disfigures: the mask shears the lips off the surrounding skin — boundary vertices get half a deformation. Binary left/right masks add a midline seam (the chin-spike artifact, visible in screenshots at weight ≈1). Minimum-norm landmark solves add needle spikes: they hit 6 target points and do anything they like in between.

✓ coefficient-space scoping

Zero the expression coefficients outside the wanted block (mouth = dims 200–349) and keep the survivors untouched.

Why it works: every retained component is a deformation the model learned from real face scans — physically coherent by construction, lips and skin moving together. Any weighted combination stays on the anatomical manifold. The basis's own regional structure does the scoping for free.

danger · the general form of this bug
Cutting a learned model's output (vertices, pixels, audio samples) breaks the correlations that made the output plausible. Cut in the model's latent/coefficient space instead, where the basis vectors already factor the signal into coherent pieces. The same mistake and the same fix show up in latent-space image editing, in audio source separation, and here — in a jaw.

Rebuilt on that principle — CVAE semantic expressions used unmasked at natural amplitude, coefficient-scoped per region, left/right pairs merged into symmetric shapes split client-side — the mouth became a mouth: teeth, intact vermilion border, natural closure. But it was still a hand-crafted approximation of what ARKit weights should mean on this head. The correct mapping was sitting in the data, waiting to be learned.

5 · Manufacturing Ground Truth (O3)

SAMPLE → RENDER → DETECT → SOLVE → BAKE

The question: what 383-vector should ARKit weight k produce on this head? Nobody has that dataset. So the pipeline manufactured it — the model interrogated itself:

1. Sample 572 expressive faces from GNM's own CVAE (all 20 semantic classes, three gains, block-scoped variants, blends). 2. Render each headless (pyrender + EGL, flat-gray skin — MediaPipe detected 28/28 probe renders, the make-or-break experiment of the whole stream). 3. Run MediaPipe FaceLandmarker on each render and its mirror → its 52 blendshape scores. That yields 1,144 (blendshape-score → known-383-expression) pairs. Zero humans, ~30 minutes, CPU.

The model itself is deliberately humble — multi-output ridge regression, 51→383, one global λ chosen on a 12-point grid by held-out face-region vertex RMSE (whole-head would let static scalp vertices dilute mouth error). Result: 0.0023 vs the zero-predictor's 0.0048 — 0.48×, clearing the pre-registered <0.6× gate. Then three finishing moves: vertex-space L/R symmetrization via the npz's mirror_indices, coefficient-block scoping per column family (chapter 4's lesson, now automated), and per-column gain calibration p95(MediaPipe)/p95(LAM) — because the training inputs are MediaPipe scores but the runtime inputs are LAM weights, and their distributions disagree (MediaPipe's jawOpen saturates near 0.55; LAM emits up to 1.0).

hack · bake the matrix into the mesh
A linear retargeter R needs no runtime. Column k of R is a 383-vector; evaluate it through the basis once and it becomes one morph-target delta named jawOpen (or whichever). 52 columns → 52 ARKit-named morph targets. The browser's name-lookup does the matrix multiply implicitly. LAM's stream went from driving 30 hand shapes to 52/52 learned channels with zero client changes.

One number refused to cooperate, and the handling of it matters more than the number: cycle-consistency (drive each learned column, re-render, re-detect, check the right channel dominates) hit 65.85% against a 75% bar. The implementer inspected the failures, showed several "failing" columns were visually correct (a rendered mouthFunnel really funnels) and that the discriminator — MediaPipe squinting at irisless gray renders — was the bottleneck, and reported the miss instead of gaming it. The reviewer later re-keyed the gate to runtime-driven channels. That honesty chain is why the numbers in this article are trustable.

6 · Puppeteering (O4)

MediaPipe's tasks-vision package runs the same FaceLandmarker in the browser via WASM — vendored locally (~10 MB with the model), no server, and its 52 blendshape outputs use the exact names baked into the GLB. Start webcam → scores → name lookup → morphs. ~16fps under headless software-GL; faster on a real GPU tab.

Verification had to be headless-reproducible, which produced the day's most elegant test rig: render a synthetic GNM face video (jaw opening and closing), encode to y4m, and feed it to Chrome's --use-file-for-fake-video-capture — the model impersonates a webcam user to test its own puppet. Result: 117/117 frames detected, mesh jawOpen tracking the fake face's mouth with oscillation range 0.211.

The adversarial reviewer then earned its keep: with a real-ish face, the puppet's eyes sat half-closed the entire session. Cause: MediaPipe reports a nonzero squint/blink baseline even on a neutral face (GNM renders have no irises, and real faces have their own bias), and eye channels the retargeter was never trained to discriminate leaked it straight into the morphs. Fix: unmap the render-blind channel families (eyeLook/eyeWide/eyeSquint/browDown), convert scores to LAM-weight units via the manifest's per-column gain and neutral baseline b0. Re-verified with the reviewer's own fake-camera rig: eyes open, mouth tracking.

So far every driver — LAM, webcam — moves a generic face. The missing person is the person.

7 · Faces From Photographs

DETECT → ALIGN → SOLVE → BAKE → BENCH

Because landmark positions are linear in identity, photo-fitting needs no optimizer: detect 2D landmarks (MediaPipe, 478→68), alternate a weak-perspective Procrustes alignment with a closed-form ridge solve over the 253 identity coefficients, five iterations. The craft is in the details that survived contact with real photos: sliding jaw correspondences (fixed jaw-contour mappings carry 44px of tangential bias; re-matching to the nearest contour point per iteration fixes it), Geman-McClure robust reweighting, and co-fitting expression that gets discarded from the export so a smiling photo yields a neutral, riggable head.

The benchmark set itself was hunted, not generated: 22 StyleGAN faces (no real person → no privacy surface) plus 9 CC-licensed Wikimedia portraits chosen for genuine diversity — an infant, an elderly judge, an Adivasi woman — each validated by the pipeline's own detector, each carrying source-URL and license in a manifest. Known metric debt, recorded rather than hidden: raw-pixel RMS isn't face-size-normalized, so full-frame faces score worse than small-in-frame ones. Inter-ocular normalization is queued.

The loop closed in the UI: a Photo → 3D button uploads to a tiny fit server, and ~60 seconds later the page reloads as that person — who then talks, puppets, and re-themes, because expression morphs are identity-independent. One photograph in, one animatable colleague out.

A minute of optimization per face is fine for a demo and wrong for a dataset. Making it a forward pass requires training — which requires knowing whether you can push gradients through a face.

8 · The Face Is a Layer (O1)

All four GNM backends — numpy, JAX, PyTorch, TensorFlow — are thin wrappers over one shared implementation built from einsum, concat, sin/cos, and an unrolled 4-joint FK loop. No in-place mutation in the forward path. No .detach(). An epsilon guard on the Rodrigues norm keeps pose gradients finite at rest. Verdict of the audit: differentiable end-to-end, w.r.t. all four inputs.

The proof ran, on CPU, in minutes: hide a random 383-dim expression, render its 68 landmarks with the numpy backend, recover it from zero-init by Adam through the PyTorch backend. Loss fell 41,000×; coefficient error in the observable subspace: 0.44%. The instructive number is the other one — 68 landmarks only observe ~89 of 383 expression directions. Sparse landmarks under-constrain a face by 4:1, which is why every serious fitting pipeline (including chapter 7's) leans on priors, and why "it hit the landmarks" never means "it found the face."

LAM's last layer is literally output_proj: Linear(512, 52) — a 512-dim audio feature crushed into ARKit's 52 slots. The O1 build amputates it and trains Linear(512, 383): audio → GNM coefficients directly, tongue dims and all. Training is feature-level (hook the 512-dim penultimate activations, cache them), so the "training run" is a linear-layer solve over cached features — minutes, not hours. Teacher targets: LAM's own 52-dim output lifted through chapter 5's learned R. Unlimited free supervision from any speech corpus.

The training-care rules were written into the workflow contract, not suggested: wav2vec2 encoder frozen always (the literature is unanimous that fine-tuning it on small data hurts); distill-first so stage 1 is never worse than the current pipeline by construction; 40-minute time-box with checkpoints and a RESUME.md; and no silent gate-lowering. Stage 2 (decoder unfreeze, geometric vertex loss through the basis-einsum) got its bounded run, tied but did not beat stage 1 — gate2_pass: false — and the pipeline shipped stage 1. A training run that declines to ship its fancier half is a training run you can trust.

9 · The Landscape Fights Back (O2)

Gradient fitting earns its complexity exactly where closed-form breaks: joint optimization of pose, full-perspective camera, identity, and per-frame expression — across video, with identity shared. Single-image results landed immediately: 7.43px on james vs the closed-form 8.40px, converging from 106px in 1500 Adam steps (48 seconds). The gradient refines what the linear solve starts.

The synthetic-video test — the only test with true ground truth, because the video is rendered from known parameters — failed spectacularly and diagnosably: reprojection ~88px against a 1.5px gate, neck pose error 59° mean, 172° max. That signature is a classic: the head-flip local minimum. Cold-started joint pose+camera optimization can reduce loss substantially with a mirrored orientation, and gradients will not climb back out. Identity and expression numbers go to garbage downstream — not because they can't fit, but because they're fitting through a flipped camera.

wild · why single-image worked and video didn't
The single-image path inherited a warm start from the closed-form Procrustes solve. The video path cold-started. Same optimizer, same losses, same model — initialization was the entire difference. The fix (in flight as this article is written): per-frame Procrustes warm-start before gradient descent, i.e., let the linear solve place you on the right side of the mountain before you start climbing.

Every result in Acts I and II — and the failure taxonomy above — came out of a machine that deserves its own chapter, because it is the reason one day held eleven shipped artifacts.

10 · The Agent Factory

The lab was built by orchestrated agent workflows in a fixed shape: research fan-out → plan (on the largest model) → parallel implementers with disjoint file ownership → adversarial review → conditional fix pass. The review stage is not ceremony; it caught the half-shut eyes (ch. 6), re-adjudicated the cycle-consistency gate (ch. 5), and re-ran implementers' own verification commands looking for lies. Implementer reports that admitted failures (65.85%, gate2) are the direct product of "no gate-gaming" being written into every prompt.

11 · The Road Ahead

Every remaining build is a seam between things that already work.

11.1 · Photo → Talking Likeness (integration, queued)

One pipeline, end to end: photo → gradient-refined identity → likeness skin (the photo→UV texture bake already prototyped through the fitted camera) → speech via the direct 383 head. Gap list, in order: a 383-dim render path — either bake per-identity morphs from head383.npz outputs or stream vertex deltas server-side (the ~2 MB sparse-basis math says a browser can even hold the whole basis); the video warm-start fix; texture wiring; a longer stage-2 window via RESUME.md; inter-ocular benchmark normalization. This is the artifact that is simultaneously the best demo, the best paper figure, and the substrate every downstream project inherits.

11.2 · The Student (photo → identity in one forward pass)

Chapter 7's fitter is a pseudo-ground-truth mint: any photo → 253 coefficients, CPU, unsupervised. Plan: mint 50–100k pairs from FFHQ-style faces, train a DINOv2-ViT-S + MLP student on the 3090 — replacing a minute of optimization with a forward pass. Under the lab's research-first stance, the accuracy ceiling rises further: MICA/Pixel3DMM supervision through a FLAME→GNM dense bridge (solved once, closed-form again), with NoW as the external scoreboard so the numbers land in the same units as the published field.

11.3 · The rest of the board

Per-person corrective deltas (likeness beyond the 253-dim span — Laplacian-smoothed, symmetric, region-masked residuals); per-user webcam neutral calibration; FaceScape registration (16,940 scans, the largest real photo↔scan corpus, research license); and the 2 MB mobile port that the payload math already guarantees.

12 · The Widening

Zoom out once. The lab's real output is not the demo — it is a substrate contract: any driver that can produce coefficients (audio model, webcam, photo fitter, game AI, hand-authored animation) can drive any surface that can sum morph targets (browser, Unreal, a phone, a server-side renderer), across any identity, because everything meets at one linear interface. The neural pieces stay swappable at the edges; the middle never changes.

That is what the linear thesis buys permanently: intelligence at the edges, algebra in the middle — and the algebra ships anywhere. The next thing that plugs into the mixing desk doesn't exist yet. The desk doesn't care.
